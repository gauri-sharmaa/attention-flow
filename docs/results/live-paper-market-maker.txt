Paper market maker on live Polymarket order books, 2026-10-14 23:46 to 2026-10-15 18:30 UTC
(about 17.7 hours of running across several machine restarts; 400 busiest markets; no real orders)

Every 10 seconds each market got paper orders of 10 shares at the best bid and/or ask.
  both    quote both sides, no signal (baseline)
  signal  quote only the side the order-book direction model favours (P(up) >= 55%: bid, <= 45%: ask)
  pull    quote both, minus the side the model expects to be run over
Fill rules: strict = only when the market trades through our price; queue = also at our price once the
resting size ahead of us has traded. Edge = midpoint move in our favour after the fill, per share, plus
Polymarket's maker rebate (~0.15c/share). Exit = 60-minute edge minus half the spread and the taker fee.

Reading: quoting without the signal loses about half a cent a share to better-informed traders
(adverse selection), clearly. The signal roughly halves that loss and brings the 60-minute edge to
about zero, but not above it, and the improvement over the baseline is not yet clearly beyond noise.
Paying to get out (half the spread + taker fee, ~1.8c) makes every strategy lose ~2c a share.
Quotes here refresh every 10 seconds; a real market maker reacts in milliseconds, so this test leans
against market making. The order-book direction call beats a coin flip clearly, but only by ~3 points
beyond simply assuming the last move continues.
